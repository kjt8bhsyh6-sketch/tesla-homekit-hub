Solar & Powerwall: smart-home tips for Tesla owners

If you have Tesla solar and a Powerwall, the Tesla app already handles a lot of energy automation. Your smart home can help with the rest: moving big loads into sunny hours and getting alerts when the grid goes down.

1. Let the car charge on solar

Charge on Solar lets your Tesla charge using only excess solar power. Tesla lists these requirements: Powerwall with solar on site (software 23.12.10 or later), vehicle software 2023.32 or later, and Tesla app 4.30.5 or later.

  1. Make sure your phone is paired with the Powerwall (Tesla app > your energy site > Settings > Phone Pairing).
  2. In the Tesla app, select your vehicle and tap Charge on Solar.
  3. Follow the prompts to set a charge limit and a charge location.
  4. Plug in at that location during the day. The car charges toward the limit only with extra solar energy.

Tip: If you don't see the option, owners report that you need to be the vehicle's owner (not an added driver) and that export or site limits can block it. Both the car and the Powerwall need a stable Wi-Fi connection.

2. Turn on Storm Watch

Storm Watch fills the Powerwall ahead of severe weather, so you can keep a low backup reserve the rest of the time. Tesla says your phone must be paired and Grid Charging must be enabled.

  1. Tesla app > Settings > Storm Watch.
  2. Turn on the Storm Watch toggle.
  3. To charge up manually before a storm, tap Schedule Event, pick a duration and tap Schedule.

3. Use Time-Based Control with your utility rates

On time-of-use rates, Time-Based Control tells the Powerwall to save stored energy for expensive hours. Set it up in the Tesla app's Powerwall modes. Then match your smart home to the same peak window:

  • Apple Home: Add a Time of Day automation at the start of peak that turns off a dehumidifier or fan on a smart plug. Add a second one at the end of peak that turns it back on.
  • Thermostat: Pre-cool or pre-heat the house just before peak starts, then set a gentle setback during peak (see the Climate guide).
  • EV: Set the car's charging schedule to finish after peak ends (see Off-peak charging).

4. Get a smart-home alert when the grid goes down

The Tesla app sends its own outage notifications. If you also want the house to react, for example by turning off non-essential plugs, you'll need a bridge like Home Assistant. Its Tesla Fleet integration can read energy-site data, and community tools such as pyPowerwall read the Powerwall locally. This is a hobbyist setup, so test it carefully.

Mark W Tebbutt (@mwt2008) found it took some work to get a Powerwall 3 to cooperate with his UK utility's "Power Down" demand-response sessions. With help from AI, he built a Home Assistant integration that coordinates the Powerwall settings and exports at a gentler, controlled rate during those sessions.

Source: @mwt2008 on X

Sam Cox (@jasonacox_sam) shared a release of pypowerwall-server that adds opt-in Home Assistant control of the Powerwall's reserve, mode, grid charging and export over MQTT. It's monitoring-only by default, which is a sensible starting point.

Source: @jasonacox_sam on X

In r/TeslaSolar, an owner couldn't find Charge on Solar. Replies point to some less obvious requirements: you have to be the vehicle owner, there can't be export limits, and the car needs to be paired under the energy site's Vehicle Charging settings. One owner sets the slider to charge to 30% from any source, then up to 80% on solar only.
