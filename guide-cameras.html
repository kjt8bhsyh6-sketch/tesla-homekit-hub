Apple Home setup, step by step

  1. In the Home app, tap the Add (+) button, then tap Add Accessory.
  2. Scan the QR code or enter the 8-digit Apple Home code (Matter devices use an 11- or 21-digit code), or hold your iPhone near the accessory.
  3. If the device was already paired to another Matter app, tap More options and select it.
  4. When asked to add the accessory to your network, tap Allow. Assign a room and a name (Siri uses this name), then tap Continue and Done.
  5. Tap the camera tile > Settings > Recording Options for When Home and When Away > Stream & Allow Recording.
  6. Turn on Activity Notifications: Home Settings > Cameras & Doorbells.

Sentry Mode at home: let your home cameras take the night shift

Sentry Mode keeps your Tesla’s cameras and sensors powered while it’s locked and in Park, and Tesla says power use may go up while it’s on. In a parked spot at home, that often isn’t worth it: swaying trees and passing neighbors can set it off all night. A better split is to let a HomeKit Secure Video camera watch the driveway or garage, and save Sentry for parking lots. These steps follow Tesla’s Owner’s Manual (Sentry Mode) and Apple’s Home app camera guide.

  1. Save your Home address. In the car, tap Navigate > Set Home. Tesla says the car must be parked within about 1,640 feet (500 m) of that spot to count as home.
  2. Turn on Exclude Home. Tap Controls > Safety > Sentry Mode and turn on Exclude Home. Sentry then won’t switch on by itself at home. Exclude Work and Exclude Favorites do the same for your other saved places.
  3. Need it for one night? Turn Sentry on by hand on the touchscreen or in the Tesla app, or say “Sentry on.” Tesla says a manual change overrides the exclusion until your next drive.
  4. Prefer a quieter Sentry? Turn off Camera-Based Detection under Controls > Safety > Sentry Mode. Tesla says the car then saves clips only when it detects a physical threat, instead of also reacting to what the cameras see.
  5. Point a home camera at the car. Add a HomeKit Secure Video camera in the Home app (see the setup steps above). Under Recording Options, choose Stream & Allow Recording and record when specific motion is detected. Apple says that means people, animals, vehicles or a package delivery, so a moving branch shouldn’t start a clip.
  6. Draw an Activity Zone around the driveway. Tap the camera tile, open its settings and tap Select Activity Zones. Outline the driveway or garage bay so motion out on the street doesn’t count. Activity Zones only work when the camera records on specific motion.
  7. Tune the alerts. In Home Settings > Cameras & Doorbells, turn on Activity Notifications for that camera. You can limit them by time of day, by whether anyone is home, or by type of motion.

Using our Garage NFC recipe? Shortcuts recipe 8, Good Night turns Sentry on at bedtime. A manual switch like that overrides Exclude Home until your next drive, so drop that step if you’d rather let the house camera handle nights at home.

Halloween note: Tesla’s new Trick or Treat mode (in Toybox, software 2026.38.3 or later) plays spooky sounds and flickers the lights when someone walks up to the parked car. While it’s on, the car warns that Sentry Mode, the security alarm and walk-away lock are disabled, and the cameras stay awake, which uses extra energy. Lock the car yourself, let the driveway camera record the evening, and turn Trick or Treat off before bed. It also switches off when you shift out of Park or the car enters Low Power Mode.

Dashcam: save the clip that matters, then get it off the car

Your Tesla’s cameras can record every drive, but Dashcam only keeps footage you save. Tesla says recordings loop and are continuously overwritten, and on a small drive that can be as little as an hour of video. Here’s how to set it up so the clip you need is still there days or years later. These steps follow Tesla’s Owner’s Manual (Dashcam, and USB Drive Requirements for Recording Videos).

  1. Use the right drive in the right port. Tesla asks for a USB drive of at least 64 GB with a sustained write speed of 4 MB/s or more that also works with USB 2.0. Many cars from about 2020 on came with a formatted drive in the glovebox. On cars built after about November 2021, the center console ports may only charge, so use the glovebox USB port.
  2. Format it in the car. Tap Controls > Safety > Format USB Drive. That erases the drive, formats it as exFAT and creates the TeslaCam folders. (Formatting on a computer works too: exFAT, FAT, ext3 or ext4, not NTFS, with a top-level folder named TeslaCam.)
  3. Turn on Dashcam and choose how clips save. Tap Controls > Safety > Dashcam. Auto saves when the car detects a safety-critical event like a collision, though Tesla warns not to rely on it to catch everything. Manual saves the last ten minutes when you tap the Dashcam icon. On Honk saves the last ten minutes when you press the horn, and you can use it together with Auto or Manual.
  4. Save it the moment something happens. Tap the Dashcam icon or honk. Missed it? In Park, open the Dashcam viewer, play the Recents clip and tap Save to keep ten minutes from anywhere in the recent footage, before it’s overwritten.
  5. Get it off the car. Saved clips land in TeslaCam/SavedClips on the drive, with six videos per clip, one from each camera. Plug the drive into a computer and copy them somewhere safe. With Premium Connectivity and View Live Camera from Mobile App turned on, you can also view and share footage in the Tesla app under Security > Dashcam Viewer.
  6. Encrypted clips? If you turned on Encrypt Dashcam Recordings, clips go to an EncryptedClips folder and won’t play on a computer directly. Tesla says to open dashcam.tesla.com in a browser and sign in with a Tesla account that was linked to the car when it recorded. The decryption happens on your computer and the clips aren’t sent to Tesla.
  7. Keep room on the drive. When the drive is full, new footage can’t be saved. Move saved clips off regularly and delete them in the viewer, or all at once under Controls > Safety > Delete Dashcam Clips.

Back it up at home: point a HomeKit Secure Video camera at the driveway or garage door (see Sentry Mode at home). The car records the road; the house camera records what happens once you pull in.

Real-world example

This week’s most-viewed Tesla story: a Florida dad was arrested in his own driveway after opening his garage door to police and charged with felony fleeing, after an unmarked car tried to pull him over and he says he never saw its lights or heard a siren. Inside Edition also reported that his Tesla’s dashcam footage was a big lift for his defense. The lesson for the rest of us: make sure Dashcam is on, and save and copy a clip you might need.
