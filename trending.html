Oct 18, 2026

1. Use Tesla’s free developer credit for your own home automations

A widely shared post points out that a Tesla Developer account comes with a monthly API credit, enough to build your own small app or hook the car up to an AI agent. The poster has theirs check at bedtime: if the battery is under 30% and the truck isn’t plugged in, send a reminder. Tesla’s developer site confirms a $10 monthly discount per account for personal use. Set a billing limit so you aren’t charged if you go over it.

Credit: @davidsven on X · No-code version: Shortcuts plug-in reminder (recipe 6)

2. Turn on Cabin Overheat Protection before summer parking

One of the most-viewed Tesla tips this week: turn on Cabin Overheat Protection so the car runs its fan or A/C after you leave it parked in the sun. Then start climate from the app just before you walk out. The poster stresses it’s an extra layer of safety, not a replacement for common sense.

Credit: @primemans on X · Step-by-step: Cabin Overheat Protection

3. Fold the mirrors automatically at a tight garage

A Model 3/Y owner folds the mirrors at a saved location (his garage, which opens through myQ). He asked Tesla to unfold them sooner, since they stay folded until the car reaches 3 mph. To set it up yourself, stop at the spot, fold the mirrors, and tap Save Location. Tesla’s manual also lists HomeLink > Auto-Fold Mirrors when Nearby. Watch the sides when you back out. Tesla also suggests turning auto-fold off if ice might build up.

Credit: @cubuffnSD on X · Related: Garage guide

4. Powershare missing? Check the Powerwall’s firmware too

An owner saw Powershare support in his car’s release notes but still couldn’t turn it on. He thinks his Powerwall needs an update, since it was on older firmware than his two Wall Connectors. A reply notes that Powerwall updates often come after the car and Wall Connector updates. Before you call support, compare the firmware versions in the Tesla app.

Credit: @mgirard on X · Credit: @marcod69420 on X · Powershare Home Backup

5. Home Assistant has a built-in Tesla integration

Another owner recommends Home Assistant’s Tesla integration for linking the car with the rest of the house. Home Assistant’s own docs say its Tesla Fleet integration checks an awake car every 10 minutes to stay within Tesla’s monthly credit for most people. Its energy-product calls (Powerwall, solar) are free. It’s a DIY route for people who want more than Shortcuts can do.

Credit: @1280MHz on X · Related: Google Home guide

Oct 17, 2026

1. Fix a stubborn Wall Connector by putting it back on Wi-Fi

An owner whose Powershare showed as enabled but wouldn’t work after a Powerwall 3 swap got a simple answer: hold the charge-handle button until the lights pulse, join the Wi-Fi the Wall Connector broadcasts, enter the password from the card in the box, and set it up again. Tesla’s own support pages describe the same steps.

Credit: @mahkus on X · Step-by-step: Wall Connector Wi-Fi

2. Tesla’s in-car Automations app shows up in Europe

Tesla’s if-this-then-that Automations app, which launched in China earlier, was spotted on a European car. You pick an event, like the driver’s door opening, and the car runs an action. It isn’t on every car yet; until it reaches yours, iPhone Shortcuts can handle many of the same ideas from your phone.

Credit: @AlJR86 on X · Try now: Shortcuts recipes

3. The Tesla app home screen now shows live weather for energy sites

For homes with Powerwall, solar, a Wall Connector or Powershare, the Tesla app’s home screen now shows site-specific weather, including time of day, clouds, rain and snow. It’s a quick way to guess how much solar you’ll get before you plan a charge.

Credit: @Cloudahay on X · Related: Solar & Powerwall

4. Apple’s Home View won’t show your Tesla solar or battery

One owner points out that Apple’s Home View doesn’t show third-party data, so Tesla solar and Powerwall levels can’t appear there. Tesla energy products aren’t Apple Home accessories, so keep the Tesla app for energy and use Apple Home for lights, locks and climate.

Credit: @EvanMFritz on X · Related: FAQ

5. Get ready for freezing mornings

A Canadian owner’s winter video covers treating door seals and using the app to deal with frozen handles before the cold hits. Another account adds the basics: keep the car plugged in at home and precondition before you leave, since cold weather cuts range.

Credit: @northern_tesla on X · Credit: @VoltForTesla on X · Related: Climate

Oct 16, 2026

1. Wall Connector Smart Charging follows your Powerwall mode

Firmware 26.34 for Powerwall and the Gen 3 Wall Connector adds Smart Charging. In Savings mode the connector leans on Low-Cost Charging (your rate plan and solar forecasts). In Self-Powered mode it prefers Charge on Solar. Pair both devices on the same Wi-Fi, then set it under Vehicle Charging in the Tesla app. Only one Wall Connector per site can use Smart Charging.

Tip: Start with a low minimum charge target and a realistic departure time so the system has room to wait for cheap power or surplus solar.

Credit: @NotATeslaApp on X · Full write-up in Solar & Powerwall

2. Model 3 and Model Y can extend home backup with Powershare

Tesla turned on Powershare Home Backup for new Model 3 and Model Y with Powerwall 3 (plus a Gen 3 or Universal Wall Connector). The car can stretch an outage backup by up to about two days on Standard packs, or over two days on Premium/Performance, per Tesla’s own estimates. Cybertruck still leads at over three days. Check Powershare Support under Software > Additional Vehicle Information on the car.

Credit: @Tesla on X · Step-by-step: Powershare Home Backup

3. Leave-home Wi-Fi can start cabin preconditioning

An iPhone Shortcuts developer suggests a personal automation: when home Wi-Fi disconnects, run immediately and call a Tesla (or third-party) precondition action. That warms or cools the cabin as you walk to the driveway, without opening the Tesla app. Add a time window so a brief Wi-Fi blip mid-day doesn’t fire it.

Credit: @GANTZZZZZZZZZ on X · Related recipes: Shortcuts

4. Enter your utility rate plan and a departure time

Once Smart Charging is on, the practical setup is simple: keep the Wall Connector and Powerwall paired, enter your utility rate plan, and set a departure time that matches your commute. The app then aims for the cheapest hours or excess solar instead of charging the moment you plug in. Works with any EV on a Gen 3 Wall Connector, not only Teslas.

Credit: @afrikha44 on X

5. Cybertruck and Powerwall 3 can back up the same house

Owners no longer have to treat the truck and the Powerwall as competing backup paths. With Powershare and Powerwall paired correctly, the truck in the garage and the Powerwall on the wall can cover the same home during an outage. Model 3/Y now join that story when they have Powershare Support enabled.

Credit: @DavidTeslaTech_ on X
