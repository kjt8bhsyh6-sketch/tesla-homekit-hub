Oct 23, 2026

1. A Tesla’s dashcam footage cleared a dad arrested in his own driveway

The most-viewed Tesla post this week: a Florida father opened his garage door to find police pointing guns at him, after an unmarked car had tried to stop him on the way home. He says he never saw lights or heard a siren, and he was charged with felony fleeing. Nearly two years later his Tesla’s recording of the drive showed the patrol car’s lights were visible only briefly, and the judge acquitted him. Dashcam only keeps what you save, so check yours is set up.

Credit: @mattvanswol on X · Credit: @InsideEdition on X · Step-by-step: Dashcam setup and backups

2. Cybertruck and Powerwall 3 can now back up the house together

An owner in hurricane country passed along InsideEVs’ report that Tesla is rolling out Powershare Home Backup for Cybertruck with a Powerwall 3 and a Wall Connector, so the truck and the Powerwall share the job instead of the truck running the house alone. He says owners get a survey email before Tesla turns it on, and that Powerwall 2 and Powerwall+ support is promised by year end. Watch for that email if you have both.

Credit: @CajunCybertruck on X · Powershare Home Backup

3. Replacing a Wall Connector? Powershare may need to be set up again

A Cybertruck owner whose Wall Connector was swapped found that Powershare stayed off until an installer could come back to re-commission it, and spent weeks getting Tesla Service, Tesla Energy and the electrician to agree on who files the work order (he says the visit costs $250 if no work order is issued). If your Wall Connector is part of a Powershare setup, ask about re-commissioning before the old unit comes off the wall.

Credit: @ivelini on X · Wall Connector setup tips

4. One Home Assistant dashboard for the car and the Powerwall

A post highlighted the community Tesla integration for Home Assistant (alandtse/tesla on GitHub), which puts a Tesla car and a Powerwall site on one dashboard: charging switch and rate, battery level, climate, Sentry and charge port for the car, plus solar, grid, home and battery power and backup reserve for the house. The poster notes it polls without waking a sleeping car, logs in with a refresh token from a third-party app, and on most cars needs the Tesla HTTP Proxy add-on to send commands. It’s a community project, not a Tesla product.

Credit: @ceguzsal on X · Project on GitHub · Solar & Powerwall guide

Oct 22, 2026

1. Leave the phone at home: your Apple Watch can be the key

An owner set up his Apple Watch as a Tesla key, went running without his phone, and the car unlocked itself when he got back. Tesla supports this officially: install the Tesla app on the watch, then tap Set Up Watch Key next to the car. Tesla’s manual adds that a watch key works with Walk-Away Door Lock, and a Home Key smart lock lets the same watch open your front door.

Credit: @IDC4GAS on X · Step-by-step: Apple Watch as your Tesla key

2. Parking in the garage? Check Trick or Treat is off

One owner loved Halloween Mode on the drive, then parked in her garage and heard strange noises she nearly called a technician about, before realizing it was her Tesla’s exterior speaker. Trick or Treat (Toybox > Halloween Mode) is made for the driveway: it plays spooky sounds through the exterior speaker and flickers the lights when someone approaches the parked car. If you park inside, make sure it’s off. Another owner points out the Tesla app can’t switch Trick or Treat on, so you set it from the car.

Credit: @GVictoriaB on X · Credit: @JuniperDriver on X · Related: Sentry Mode at home

3. Put your Wall Connector’s live charging data on a screen

An owner built a TV screensaver that shows his Gen 3 Wall Connector live: the current charging session, power and lifetime kWh, next to the weather. You don’t have to write code to get similar data. Home Assistant’s Tesla Wall Connector integration reads a Gen 3 Wi-Fi Wall Connector over your home network every 30 seconds, with sensors for session energy, lifetime energy, status, vehicle current and Wi-Fi signal, and the lifetime sensor can feed its Energy dashboard. It needs the Wall Connector on Wi-Fi.

Credit: @xenappblog on X · Get your Wall Connector on Wi-Fi

4. Charging on spare solar without a Powerwall

Tesla’s own Charge on Solar needs a Powerwall with solar on site. An owner without one shared a do-it-yourself route: a small local Mac app that reads his solar inverter over Modbus TCP and raises or lowers the Tesla’s charging current (5–32 A in his setup) to match the excess solar, with no subscription. It’s a hobby project, not a Tesla feature, so check that your inverter allows local access before you try anything similar.

Credit: @ev_downunder on X · Solar & Powerwall guide

Oct 21, 2026

1. Frozen charge cable? There’s a heater button for that

One of the most-viewed Tesla tips this week: when ice locks the charging cable in the port, don’t force it. Go to Service on the touchscreen and turn on the charge port heater to free the plug. Tesla’s manuals back it up and add a detail: on U.S. Model 3 and Model Y the button is called Inlet Heater, and Defrost Car in the app or a Precondition schedule also helps thaw (or prevent) a frozen latch.

Credit: @liorsela on X · Step-by-step: Frozen charge cable

2. Owners are racing to stay off grid on solar, Powerwall and Powershare

A group of Powerwall owners unplugged from the grid on Oct 17 to see who could run longest on solar and batteries alone, posting live updates. One Illinois participant reported more than 75 hours off grid with his Model Y recharging from sunshine during the day, but also found that at night the system stopped charging the car from the Powerwalls with a “Powerwall energy level low” message, even with plenty stored. Tesla’s support page explains the setting involved: during an outage, Powerwall charges the car only while it’s above the vehicle charging threshold you set in the Tesla app, slows charging to keep the house running, and can share extra solar with the car. Plug in during midday sun if the grid is down.

Credit: @TeslaHype on X · Credit: @JoshuaThePilot on X · Powershare Home Backup

3. Put a Tesla control on the iPhone Action button

With iPhone Action button “tier lists” making the rounds, Tesla owners chimed in with their pick: one presses it to precondition the car, and another noticed it can reach Tesla controls like climate and locks. Apple’s steps: Settings > Action Button, swipe to Shortcut, then choose a shortcut. Pick a one-action Tesla shortcut such as Precondition or Defrost, and the button works without opening the app.

Credit: @dino_vivo on X · Credit: @CallEllie2help on X · Related: Shortcuts recipes

4. Turn on Walk-Away Door Lock, and decide what happens at home

Two posts this week make the case for Walk-Away Door Lock: walk off with your hands full and the car locks itself. Find it under Controls > Locks. Two details from Tesla’s manual: it works with a phone key, paired key fob or Apple Watch key, but not the key card. And if you’d rather the car stay unlocked in your own garage, tick Exclude Home after you set Home in navigation. Tesla still says it’s your job to make sure the car is locked.

Credit: @DigitalAssetsUS on X · Credit: @alex_miller_ai on X · Related: Smart locks guide

Oct 20, 2026

1. Let the Powerwall fill itself up before a storm

Tesla Energy’s widely shared clip has a Powerwall owner in Hawaii explaining how Storm Watch tops off the battery before a storm arrives, so the family is covered if the power goes out. A reply adds the catch: it only works with Grid Charging turned on. Tesla’s support page agrees. It also says your phone must be paired, the Powerwall charges to full when a local warning like a hurricane, tornado, winter storm, high wind or flood warning is issued, and it goes back to normal afterward.

Credit: @teslaenergy on X · Credit: @jeremycancio on X · Step-by-step: Storm Watch

2. Trick or Treat mode turns the driveway Tesla into a Halloween prop

A popular demo shows Tesla’s new Trick or Treat mode in action: the parked car plays spooky sounds and flickers its lights when someone walks up. Owners point out the trade-off. While it’s on, Sentry Mode, the security alarm and walk-away lock are off, and keeping the cameras awake uses extra energy. Lock the car yourself and let a home camera cover the driveway that night.

Credit: @alphafox on X · Credit: @XploreFutureX on X · Credit: @MissGossip on X · Sentry Mode at home

3. Skip Sentry at home and let the house camera watch the car

An owner says Sentry at home wakes him up to 80 alerts from trees blowing around while the car flashes at his neighbor’s house. Another owner suggests turning Sentry off in a safe home garage to save a few miles of range overnight. Tesla has a setting for exactly this: Exclude Home, under Controls > Safety > Sentry Mode. Pair it with a driveway camera in Apple Home.

Credit: @DJ_DSFU on X · Credit: @IzharTesla on X · Step-by-step: Sentry Mode at home

4. Test your phone key before you leave the delivery center

A delivery-week tip: before you drive off, walk away from the car with your phone, come back, and make sure the phone key unlocks it. If it doesn’t, the advisor is right there to help. The next morning in your own driveway, you’re on your own with the key card. Every phone-based automation on this site assumes your phone key works, so it’s worth checking first.

Credit: @GardenSnakess on X · Related: Tesla automations

Oct 19, 2026

1. Let the car warm itself on wall power before you leave

A heavily viewed thread of “features most owners miss” puts the morning schedule near the top: tell the car what time you leave, and it warms the cabin and battery and finishes charging right on time. The poster’s point is that a schedule while plugged in uses wall power, while tapping climate on later pulls from the battery. Tesla’s Winter Driving Tips back up the plugged-in part. The feature now lives under Controls > Schedule.

Credit: @Eric_Smith08 on X · Credit: @Heyabhi29 on X · Step-by-step: Scheduled Precondition

2. Powershare with two Wall Connectors? Give each its own circuit

An owner troubleshooting Powershare reports that Tesla told him it doesn’t work with Wall Connectors sharing one circuit or a power-sharing group. Each one needs its own dedicated circuit. Tesla’s install docs line up: they say each Wall Connector needs its own branch circuit, and that power-sharing features aren’t available on a Universal Wall Connector installed for Powershare. Ask your installer before you plan a shared setup.

Credit: @haiway on X · Powershare Home Backup

3. A Wall Connector can fit a smaller panel at a lower setting

An electrician shared a garage Wall Connector install that needed no new panel: the existing panel had room once a few circuits were doubled up, and the charger was set to 24 amps to match the home’s service. Tesla’s install manual lists that option: a 30 A breaker gives up to 24 A of charging, and the breaker size is set during commissioning. A 60 A breaker gives the full 48 A. Leave the sizing to a licensed electrician.

Credit: @formelectric on X · Related: Charging guide

4. Have a gate? Check before you swap HomeLink for myQ

An owner says his older Model X opened both his gate and his garage door through HomeLink, but his new Cybertruck can’t open the gate even with a paid myQ subscription. He’s asking Tesla to add gate openers. If your driveway has a gate, check whether your car and opener support it before you count on the built-in garage button for both.

Credit: @marcXXII on X · Related: Garage guide

A widely shared post points out that a Tesla Developer account comes with a monthly API credit, enough to build your own small app or hook the car up to an AI agent. The poster has theirs check at bedtime: if the battery is under 30% and the truck isn’t plugged in, send a reminder. 2. Turn on Cabin Overheat Protection before summer parking

One of the most-viewed Tesla tips this week: turn on Cabin Overheat Protection so the car runs its fan or A/C after you leave it parked in the sun. Then start climate from the app just before you walk out. The poster stresses it’s an extra layer of safety, not a replacement for common sense.

Credit: @primemans on X · Step-by-step: Cabin Overheat Protection

3. Fold the mirrors automatically at a tight garage

A Model 3/Y owner folds the mirrors at a saved location (his garage, which opens through myQ). He asked Tesla to unfold them sooner, since they stay folded until the car reaches 3 mph. To set it up yourself, stop at the spot, fold the mirrors, and tap Save Location. Tesla’s manual also lists HomeLink > Auto-Fold Mirrors when Nearby. Watch the sides when you back out. Tesla also suggests turning auto-fold off if ice might build up.

4. Powershare missing? Check the Powerwall’s firmware too

An owner saw Powershare support in his car’s release notes but still couldn’t turn it on. He thinks his Powerwall needs an update, since it was on older firmware than his two Wall Connectors. A reply notes that Powerwall updates often come after the car and Wall Connector updates. Before you call support, compare the firmware versions in the Tesla app.

Credit: @mgirard on X · Credit: @marcod69420 on X · Powershare Home Backup

5. Home Assistant has a built-in Tesla integration

Another owner recommends Home Assistant’s Tesla integration for linking the car with the rest of the house. Home Assistant’s own docs say its Tesla Fleet integration checks an awake car every 10 minutes to stay within Tesla’s monthly credit for most people. Its energy-product calls (Powerwall, solar) are free. It’s a DIY route for people who want more than Shortcuts can do.

An owner whose Powershare showed as enabled but wouldn’t work after a Powerwall 3 swap got a simple answer: hold the charge-handle button until the lights pulse, join the Wi-Fi the Wall Connector broadcasts, enter the password from the card in the box, and set it up again. Tesla’s own support pages describe the same steps.

For homes with Powerwall, solar, a Wall Connector or Powershare, the Tesla app’s home screen now shows site-specific weather, including time of day, clouds, rain and snow. It’s a quick way to guess how much solar you’ll get before you plan a charge.

Oct 16, 2026

1. Wall Connector Smart Charging follows your Powerwall mode

Firmware 26.34 for Powerwall and the Gen 3 Wall Connector adds Smart Charging. In Savings mode the connector leans on Low-Cost Charging (your rate plan and solar forecasts). In Self-Powered mode it prefers Charge on Solar. Pair both devices on the same Wi-Fi, then set it under Vehicle Charging in the Tesla app. Only one Wall Connector per site can use Smart Charging.

Tip: Start with a low minimum charge target and a realistic departure time so the system has room to wait for cheap power or surplus solar.

Credit: @NotATeslaApp on X · Full write-up in Solar & Powerwall

2. Model 3 and Model Y can extend home backup with Powershare

Tesla turned on Powershare Home Backup for new Model 3 and Model Y with Powerwall 3 (plus a Gen 3 or Universal Wall Connector). The car can stretch an outage backup by up to about two days on Standard packs, or over two days on Premium/Performance, per Tesla’s own estimates. Cybertruck still leads at over three days. Check Powershare Support under Software > Additional Vehicle Information on the car.

Credit: @Tesla on X · Step-by-step: Powershare Home Backup

3. Leave-home Wi-Fi can start cabin preconditioning

An iPhone Shortcuts developer suggests a personal automation: when home Wi-Fi disconnects, run immediately and call a Tesla (or third-party) precondition action. That warms or cools the cabin as you walk to the driveway, without opening the Tesla app. Add a time window so a brief Wi-Fi blip mid-day doesn’t fire it.

Credit: @GANTZZZZZZZZZ on X · Related recipes: Shortcuts

4. Enter your utility rate plan and a departure time

Once Smart Charging is on, the practical setup is simple: keep the Wall Connector and Powerwall paired, enter your utility rate plan, and set a departure time that matches your commute. The app then aims for the cheapest hours or excess solar instead of charging the moment you plug in. Works with any EV on a Gen 3 Wall Connector, not only Teslas.

Credit: @afrikha44 on X

5. Cybertruck and Powerwall 3 can back up the same house

Owners no longer have to treat the truck and the Powerwall as competing backup paths. With Powershare and Powerwall paired correctly, the truck in the garage and the Powerwall on the wall can cover the same home during an outage. Model 3/Y now join that story when they have Powershare Support enabled.

Credit: @DavidTeslaTech_ on X
