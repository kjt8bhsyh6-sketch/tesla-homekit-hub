Smart Locks: Apple Home guide

⬇ Download free PDF: HomeKit Smart Lock Setup Guide

Locks that support Apple Home Key let you tap an iPhone or Apple Watch to unlock, much like Phone Key on your Tesla.

How to choose

  • Look for "Apple Home Key" on the box if you want tap-to-unlock in Wallet. Not every Home-compatible lock supports it.
  • Fingerprint and keypad models give non-iPhone guests a way in.
  • Wi-Fi models can be controlled remotely; otherwise rely on your home hub.

Apple Home setup, step by step

  1. In the Home app, tap the Add (+) button, then tap Add Accessory.
  2. Scan the QR code or enter the 8-digit Apple Home code (Matter devices use an 11- or 21-digit code), or hold your iPhone near the accessory.
  3. If the device was already paired to another Matter app, tap More options and select it.
  4. When asked to add the accessory to your network, tap Allow. Assign a room and a name (Siri uses this name), then tap Continue and Done.
  5. If the lock supports Home Key, a home key is added to Apple Wallet automatically. Choose Express Mode or Require Face ID or Passcode.
  6. Turn on suggested automations like Lock After Door Closes and Lock When Leaving Home.
  7. Guest codes: touch and hold the lock > Accessory Settings > Manage Access > Add Guest.

Use your Apple Watch as your Tesla key (and your front-door key)

Your Apple Watch can unlock and lock your Tesla the same way your phone key does, so you can leave the iPhone at home for a run or a walk with the dog. Add a Home Key lock and the same wrist opens the front door too. These steps follow Tesla’s owner’s manual and support pages and Apple’s Apple Watch and home key guides.

  1. Check what you need. Tesla lists an Apple Watch Series 6, Apple Watch SE (2nd generation), Apple Watch Ultra (1st generation) or newer, running watchOS 11 or later; Tesla app 4.39.5 or later; and vehicle software 2024.44.25 or later. Update your iPhone and watch first.
  2. Make sure your phone key already works. In the car, check Controls > Safety > Allow Mobile Access is on, and that the Tesla app on your iPhone has Bluetooth on and Location set to Always.
  3. Put the Tesla app on your watch. Open the Apple Watch app on your iPhone, tap My Watch > Apps, scroll to Available Apps, and tap Install next to Tesla. (By default the watch may already have installed it.)
  4. Stand next to the car and pair the watch. Open the Tesla app on your Apple Watch and tap Set Up Watch Key, then follow the prompts on the watch and the car’s touchscreen.
  5. If pairing fails, Tesla says to stay right next to the car, and if it’s already connected to several phone keys, turn off Bluetooth on your iPhone for a moment so the watch can connect. (A Model Y can connect to three phone keys at a time.)
  6. Let it lock itself. Turn on Controls > Locks > Walk-Away Door Lock. Tesla’s manual says an authenticated Apple Watch key works with it. Tick Exclude Home if you want the car to stay unlocked in your own garage. Tesla still says it’s your job to make sure the car is locked.
  7. Add the house. With a lock that supports Apple home key, the Home app adds a home key to Wallet when you pair the lock. Apple says that with a home key on your iPhone or Apple Watch and Express Mode on, you just hold the device near the lock to open it. See the setup steps above.

Also on your wrist: Tesla’s watch app can open the frunk and charge port, show the battery level and turn on climate, so you can warm the car from the kitchen without finding your phone.

Good to know: Keep your key card with you as a backup; Tesla says it’s what you use if your phone key’s battery dies. Tesla also warns not to leave a paired phone in the parked car, and if a key is detected inside, Walk-Away Door Lock won’t lock.
